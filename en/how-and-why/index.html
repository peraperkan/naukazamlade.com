Why do we call the Moon the Moon? A story of the sky, language and measuring time

Jupiter has Europa, Io, Ganymede and Callisto. Saturn has Titan and Encelad. And Earth has — the Moon. At first glance, our nearest celestial neighbour seems to have missed out on a special name while all the others received more interesting ones. Yet the Moon itself provided the starting point for naming other bodies of this kind. Its name connects the history of astronomy with the history of language: long before telescopes and space probes, changes in its appearance helped people count days and follow the passage of time.

How does the brain change as we learn? What neuroplasticity really means

When we first try to play a melody, solve a physics problem or speak a sentence in a new language, the task is slow and demands attention. Practice makes it easier. That change is more than a feeling: experience affects how nerve cells communicate and how whole networks take part in a task. We call this capacity neuroplasticity. It remains with us throughout life, although its form and extent depend on age, brain region and the skill involved.
