Can a sound we cannot hear affect our mood?

A faint vibration runs through an old building's ventilation system. We cannot clearly hear it, yet the space feels uncomfortable. Is that only expectation, or can very low frequencies affect experience? A small laboratory study published in 2026 found links between infrasound exposure, irritability, ratings of music and cortisol levels. The finding is intriguing, but it does not prove that “haunted houses” have an acoustic explanation.

Japan has sent a spacecraft to sample a Martian moon: what could MMX reveal about Phobos?

An H3 rocket has sent Japan’s MMX spacecraft towards Mars. If the next stages succeed, it will examine both Martian moons at close range, put a rover on Phobos and return surface material to Earth. The sample could help resolve an old debate: are these moons captured asteroids or debris from an impact on Mars?

How can diamond and graphite contain the same element?

Both diamond and graphite consist of carbon, but their atoms are connected differently. In diamond each atom connects to four neighbours in a strong three-dimensional network. In graphite the atoms form layers, with much weaker bonds between them. Those layers can slide, allowing graphite to leave marks on paper, whereas diamond is extremely hard. Atomic arrangement can therefore change a material’s properties as dramatically as changing its chemical composition.
