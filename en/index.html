A wobbling asteroid: what Lucy learned about Donaldjohanson

In images from NASA's Lucy spacecraft, asteroid Donaldjohanson looks like an elongated peanut: two uneven lobes joined by a narrow neck. Its shape is only the beginning of the story. An analysis published in June 2026 shows that the roughly eight-kilometre body does not simply turn around one steady axis; it slowly tumbles. Its surface also preserves chemical evidence of contact with liquid water, probably from a time before the present asteroid formed. How can a spacecraft discover all this during a brief flyby?

How does a vaccine use immune memory?

After encountering a particular antigen, the immune system can retain memory cells. Vaccination uses this ability by presenting an antigen, or instructions for producing it, without the risks of the full disease itself. A later encounter with the pathogen can consequently trigger a faster and more effective response. Protection depends on the vaccine, the pathogen and the individual, so it is not always complete or equally long-lasting. Additional doses help strengthen or maintain the response for some vaccines.
