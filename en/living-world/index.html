The unseen residents of tree bark: how microbes change the path of methane

When we look at a tree, we usually think of leaves taking up carbon dioxide through photosynthesis. Researchers have looked at the bark as well. It houses communities of microorganisms that use gases moving through the trunk or arriving from the air. An Australian team reported in 2026 that, when oxygen is available, these microbes can consume methane, hydrogen and carbon monoxide. A tree is therefore also a habitat whose inhabitants affect the exchange of gases between soil and atmosphere.
